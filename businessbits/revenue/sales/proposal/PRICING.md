# Pricing

Rules for setting a price in a one or two person consultancy or agency. The business's own rates, floor and terms are in its profile.

## Choose the basis

It depends on the service:

- **Day rate** for training and workshops, and for open-ended advice nobody can scope yet. Agree a fixed figure for a set number of days before starting.
- **Fixed price** for a specific deliverable, such as a workflow or an app. A day rate punishes the business for getting faster.
- **Retainer** for an ongoing system where maintenance is part of the system, such as a custom app or several workflows the client relies on. Name the systems, the hours and what's excluded.

One proposal can mix them: a fixed price for the build, and a separate retainer for keeping it running.

If the work doesn't fit the profile's standard prices, price it custom. Don't force it into the nearest tier.

## Check the floor

Divide the fixed price by the estimated days. That effective day rate must clear the profile's floor. If it doesn't, change the scope or the price, not the estimate.

## Anchor on value

Write one line that sets the price against the cost of the problem, using the buyer's own numbers: people x hours a week on the problem x loaded hourly cost, against the fee. If the buyer hasn't given numbers, ask for them. Never invent them.

## Discounts

A discount is a deliberate purchase of something: usually a case study or a reference customer. Name it as what it is (an early-client, case-study, reference-customer, or friends and family rate), show the full price next to it, time-box it, write the price it steps up to at signing, and get any case-study permission in writing. Raising an anchored client's price later is the hard conversation; name the step-up now.

## When the buyer pushes back on price

Offer a lever before cutting the figure: a later start or lower priority, or a simpler scope. Two things are never traded, unless the profile says otherwise: the floor day rate, and the acceptance test. Keep to anything else the profile lists as not for trading.

## Read the answer

An instant yes with no negotiation means the price was too low. Win the work, and raise the next quote.

## Terms

- Payment by milestones for builds (half on signing, half on acceptance is common); fixed sessions paid before delivery; retainers monthly in advance.
- Third-party costs (software, model usage, travel) billed at cost to the client's own accounts where possible.
- VAT stated correctly for the business's registration status.

## Before quoting, confirm

Scope from discovery, case-study rights, VAT, and which parts reuse the business's existing work versus work built new for this client.
