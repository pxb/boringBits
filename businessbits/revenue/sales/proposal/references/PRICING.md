# Pricing

Rules for setting a price in a one or two person consultancy or agency. The business's own rates, floor and terms are in its profile.

## Choose the basis

It depends on the service:

- **Day rate** for training and workshops, and for open-ended advice nobody can scope yet. Agree a fixed figure for a set number of days before starting.
- **Fixed price** for a specific deliverable, such as a workflow or an app. A day rate punishes the business for getting faster.
- **Retainer** for an ongoing system where maintenance is part of the system, such as a custom app or several workflows the client relies on. Name the systems, the hours and what's excluded.

One proposal can mix them: a fixed price for the build, and a separate retainer for keeping it running.

If the work doesn't fit the profile's standard prices, price it custom. Don't force it into the nearest tier.

## Check the floor

Divide the fixed price by the estimated days. That effective day rate must clear the profile's floor. If it doesn't, change the scope or the price, not the estimate.

## Anchor on value

Write one line that sets the price against the cost of the problem, using the buyer's own numbers: people x hours a week on the problem x loaded hourly cost, against the fee. If the buyer hasn't given numbers, ask for them. Never invent them.

## Options and extras

Choose one shape for the extras and keep it across the whole document:

- **Counted inside an item.** The extra sits indented under the item it belongs to, with its own payment term, and the item closes on a row labelled `Subtotal`. The item's own price is the same size as its extras; the subtotal is the larger figure because it is the sum.
- **One optional group.** Every extra is listed once, under a heading that names its cadence (for example "Optional, per event"), with the group price beside the heading and each line underneath. The build group gets a heading too ("Build, one-off"). The section closes on one all-in figure that includes the build and every option.

Mixing the two gives the same work two prices. Options sit outside the headline total, and the document says so in those words.

When a priced line is split into options, everything the original line promised lands in one of the options, and the combined price of taking them all is stated.

## When a line changes

- **The agreed total holds.** If one line's price changes, move another line by the difference and tell the user which line moved and by how much. A total that follows its lines turns every edit into a renegotiation.
- **Totals are added up from the lines, never typed.** A typed total drifts the first time a line changes.
- **Every place the line appears moves with it:** the cover price, the item's subtotal and its list, the total, the timeline stage that delivers it, and the exclusions, which may now deny what the line buys. Re-read each one, and check the old figure appears nowhere.

## Discounts

A discount is a deliberate purchase of something: usually a case study or a reference customer. Name it as what it is (an early-client, case-study, reference-customer, or friends and family rate), show the full price next to it, time-box it, write the price it steps up to at signing, and get any case-study permission in writing. Raising an anchored client's price later is the hard conversation; name the step-up now.

## When the buyer pushes back on price

Lead with the full offer, everything included, then show the trim as a ladder the buyer can pick from: all in, one option off, all options off. Never open at the stripped price and sell upwards. Options sold as a pair come off as a pair, and the proposal says so.

If they still push back, offer a lever before cutting the figure: a later start or lower priority, or a simpler scope. Two things are never traded, unless the profile says otherwise: the floor day rate, and the acceptance test. Keep to anything else the profile lists as not for trading.

## Read the answer

An instant yes with no negotiation means the price was too low. Win the work, and raise the next quote.

## Terms

- Payment by milestones for builds (half on signing, half on acceptance is common); fixed sessions paid before delivery; retainers monthly in advance.
- The payment term matches the shape of the work. Work that repeats (per event, per batch, per month of content) is paid per unit on delivery. Milestones describe a one-off build; on a repeating line they read as a billing cycle nobody offered.
- Costs the client pays directly (hosting, card fees, software) go in one note beside the total, not hidden in the terms. They are the answer to "why does it cost this much to run".
- Third-party costs (software, model usage, travel) billed at cost to the client's own accounts where possible.
- VAT stated correctly for the business's registration status.

## Before quoting, confirm

Scope from discovery, case-study rights, VAT, and which parts reuse the business's existing work versus work built new for this client.
