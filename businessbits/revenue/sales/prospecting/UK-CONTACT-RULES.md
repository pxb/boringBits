# UK contact rules for business prospecting

A working summary of the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations (PECR) and UK GDPR for one or two person businesses contacting other businesses. Not legal advice. The ICO's direct marketing guidance at ico.org.uk is the authority; check it before relying on this, because the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 is changing parts of it, including higher fines for PECR breaches.

## Who you're contacting decides the email rule

| Recipient | Marketing email allowed | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Limited company, LLP, plc, Scottish partnership, public body (a corporate subscriber) | Yes, without prior consent | Identify yourself and give a simple way to opt out in every message |
| Sole trader or ordinary English, Welsh or Northern Irish partnership (an individual subscriber) | Only with consent or a soft opt-in | Soft opt-in needs an existing customer or a sale negotiation, similar services, and an opt-out offered at collection and in every message |

Record the legal form on the organisation, and the basis on the contact, before drafting any email.

## Phone

Screen every number against the Telephone Preference Service (individuals) and the Corporate Telephone Preference Service (companies) before a sales call, unless the person has told you they're happy to be called. The default sequence in the prospecting skill only calls after a reply.

## Personal data about the people you contact

- **Lawful basis:** legitimate interests usually covers business prospecting. Write a short legitimate interests assessment once: the purpose, why it's necessary, and why it doesn't override the person's interests (a work role, work contact details, a relevant offer).
- **Privacy notice (Article 14):** when you collect someone's details from somewhere other than them, tell them who you are, what you hold, where it came from, why, and how to object. Do it at the first message, and at the latest within one month. One line and a link to a privacy page is the usual way.
- **Objections are absolute:** anyone who objects to marketing goes on the suppression list as permanent.
- **Keep only what you need:** work contact details, role, sources. Nothing personal.
- **ICO fee:** holding a prospect list usually means the business must pay the ICO data protection fee.

## Platforms

LinkedIn and other platforms have their own rules on messaging and automation. Follow them. Treat platform messages with the same care as email: relevant, identified, easy to decline.
