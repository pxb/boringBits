# Research method

Run these stages in order for every account. Free sources before paid ones. When a stage finds nothing, write `Not found` and say what was tried. Stop and label; never invent.

## 1. Suppression gate

Check the organisation against the suppression list, current clients, open enquiries and open opportunities, by name with legal suffixes removed and by company number. Re-check after stage 2, because a parent or sister company can be a client.

## 2. Legal entity

From the company register: exact name, number, status, incorporation date, registered office, officers with appointment dates, and the parent company where the register shows one. A company in liquidation, dissolving or with overdue filings is a reason to drop it.

Don't state turnover you can't source. Don't treat a personal email domain as evidence of identity.

## 3. Domain

Find the trading website and the domain its people use for email. Confirm the domain resolves. Check that the site names the same company, ideally by company number in the footer or legal page.

Lookup tools that resolve a domain from a company name can return a similarly named company abroad. Check the country and name against the register.

## 4. Named person

Find the person who owns the problem the business solves. In a small company that's usually the owner or managing director; in a larger one, the head of the function concerned. Sources, in order: the company's own team page, register officers, a contact finder tool if the profile has one, recent news.

A generic inbox (info@, sales@, hello@) is not a contact. A phone number against a generic inbox is not a contact. If there's no named person, drop the account and pick another.

## 5. Two checks on the contact

1. **Deliverable:** an email verifier says the mailbox exists. `Accept-all` domains can't confirm this, so treat them as unconfirmed.
2. **Current:** a live source shows the person still holds the role: an active register appointment, the team page, or news from the last six months.

Label: both pass = `deliverable-and-current`; one passes = `verify-before-sending`; no email = `none`. A mailbox stays live after someone leaves, so deliverable alone is never enough.

## 6. LinkedIn

Accept a profile only when the result shows the person's full name and the company. Never construct or guess a profile URL. Otherwise write `none`.

## 7. Why now

One recent, dated, sourced event beats three vague ones. Carry the date. If there's no event, say the need is standing and label it **Likely**.

## 8. Fit and angle

Score against the profile's buyer description and the pattern of past wins. Write the angle (why this business, why this first offer) for the user only. It never goes into a message.

## 9. Proof match

Pick the closest case or result from the profile. Link it. Never offer to "send something over" instead of linking proof that exists.

## 10. Cost

Record each paid call (tool, count, cost) against the account. Stop at the profile's monthly budget and tell the user.
